01

“Does this colour
actually suit me?”

Almost nobody can answer that reliably. Seasonal colour analysis is usually done by eye, in inconsistent lighting, by someone guessing. The same person gets told “autumn” by one stylist and “winter” by the next.

And fit advice is worse — reduced to a body-shape label with a list of rules attached, when what actually matters is the ratio between your shoulders, waist and hips, and what balances it.

A photo

03

Colour, measured
not guessed.

RGB distance is perceptually wrong — two colours the same numeric distance apart can look nothing alike. So every colour here is converted to CIE L*a*b*, a space built so equal numeric distance means equal perceived difference, then compared with CIEDE2000.

Undertone comes from the a*/b* axes rather than crude RGB subtraction, which is why it holds up across different lighting. Depth comes from L*, real perceptual lightness.

CIEDE2000 implementation validated against the Sharma et al. reference dataset — all test pairs match to four decimal places.

04

Five builds.
Five strategies.

Shape isn't a verdict, it's a starting point. Each classification carries a balancing strategy — what to add, what to avoid, and why.

Rectangle

Little natural taper. Build structure with layering and texture.

Inverted triangle

Chest wider than hip. Add visual weight below; skip shoulder structure.

Triangle

Hip wider than chest. Structured shoulders, darker straight bottoms.

Oval

Waist widest. Vertical lines, open layers, tonal dressing.

Hourglass

Defined waist. Preserve it — don't bury it under uniform volume.
